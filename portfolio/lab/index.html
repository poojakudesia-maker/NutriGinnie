NutriPing

A dietitian's PDF, turned into a week of Indian meals and tonight's grocery list.

The problem

A dietitian hands you a chart. It says 1,400 kcal and lists six dishes. Then you're on your own at 7 PM, trying to work out what to cook tomorrow and what to buy for it. Calorie apps want you to log Western portions of food you don't eat. None of them read the plan you already paid for.

What I built

  • A profile wizard that works out BMI, BMR (Mifflin-St Jeor), TDEE and a safe deficit. If your dietitian already gave you a number, you can type it in instead.
  • Upload a diet chart as a PDF or DOCX. Claude pulls out every dish, and you check the list before anything is saved. Scanned and handwritten charts go to Claude as images so they still get read.
  • Paste a YouTube or Instagram recipe link. YouTube captions are fetched automatically, and Claude turns the recipe into ingredients and macros.
  • A 7-day plan built from your own recipes with no AI call at all. Claude only writes a plan when you haven't given it anything to work with.
  • Swap a single meal, a grocery list combined across the day's meals, and a nightly reminder with tomorrow's plan.

What production taught me

The first version ran on Next.js and Vercel. Hobby-plan cron limits and serverless timeouts kept getting in the way, so I rewrote it in Laravel on ordinary PHP hosting. Plan generation still timed out with a 504, so it now runs as a background job and the screen polls until it's done.

Delivery was meant to be WhatsApp. Meta rejects template messages that contain line breaks, so a whole day of meals had to fit on one line in under 1,000 characters. App Review asks for a privacy policy before anything gets sent. I built all of it, then switched the nightly plan to email so people could start using it while the review is pending. The design lesson: the channel is part of the product, and somebody else controls it.

ApexSurge

Most people who read self-improvement books never apply them. This turns a book into a daily experiment.

The problem

Summary apps like Headway made it faster to get through books. They didn't make anyone change. You finish Deep Work, feel motivated for a weekend, and on Monday your calendar looks the same as last week. The gap is between knowing something and doing it, and consuming more content doesn't close it.

What I'm building

  • You pick goals in four areas (career, personal growth, communication and productivity), and Claude suggests books that fit them.
  • Daily missions of 5 to 15 minutes. Each one takes a single idea from the book and gives you a small real-world test to try today.
  • An 8-week roadmap with weekly reviews, plus an AI coach that answers questions using only the books you've worked through.
  • Roleplay for the hard parts, like practising a difficult conversation before you have it.
  • Missions read aloud with the browser's built-in speech, so audio costs nothing to run.

Design decisions so far

I measure a mission by whether you tried it, not whether you read it. I kept running costs to $3–7 a month at MVP scale, and about $20 at a thousand users, by caching book summaries and using free browser speech instead of a paid voice API. That means I can test it with real people before charging anyone.
